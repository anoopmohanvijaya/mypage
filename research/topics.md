My research focuses on understanding complex fluid dynamical systems through a combination of high-fidelity experiments, numerical simulations, and data-driven modelling. Below are three ongoing research directions I am actively pursuing.

---

## 1. **Instabilities and Energy Pathways in Inertia–Gravity Waves**

I study the onset, evolution, and saturation of instabilities in inertia–gravity waves using controlled laboratory experiments and theoretical analysis.  
This work aims to clarify how wave–mean flow interactions influence large-scale geophysical circulations, with applications in ocean mixing, atmospheric dynamics, and climate modelling.

**Key topics:**
- Wave breaking and secondary instabilities  
- Energy cascades in stratified–rotating flows  
- Comparison of laboratory observations with idealized theory  

---

## 2. **Bistable Jet Regimes in Rotating and Stratified Systems**

This project investigates bistability, hysteresis, and regime switching in eastward jets generated in rotating fluids.  
By combining high-resolution numerical simulations and reduced-order models, I aim to understand how environmental parameters control transitions between multiple equilibrium states.

**Key topics:**
- Nonlinear dynamics of zonal jets  
- Role of topography, rotation rate, and forcing  
- Early indicators of regime transitions using data-driven diagnostics  

---

## 3. **Machine-Learning-Enhanced Sub-Grid Scale (SGS) Parameterization**

I develop machine-learning approaches to improve the representation of unresolved turbulence in numerical models.  
This effort focuses on constructing interpretable, physically consistent SGS closures that integrate seamlessly into existing geophysical flow solvers.

**Key topics:**
- Physics-informed neural networks for turbulence  
- Stable and conservative learning architectures  
- Hybrid data–theory SGS models for large-eddy simulations
